SECTION 0 · READ THIS FIRST
Why this edition exists
This replaces the 08 October Round 4 order. The requirements are the same; what changed is how
they are written.
The teardown finally came out right, and the reason was simple: it was described like a film, shot by
shot, with timings, camera moves, the exact light, the exact shadows, the materials, and how the
moment should feel. Vague lines like "make it premium" produced generic work. Precise direction
produced the best section on the site.
So every section in this edition is written the same way. Read each one as a director's script. Build
exactly what the script describes, frame by frame. When the script gives a time, a colour, an easing
or a size, that is the value. When it describes a feeling, every visual decision you make on your own
must serve that feeling.
Non-negotiable: do not "interpret" a script into something easier. Do not skip a shot because it
is hard. Do not replace a shot with your own idea without telling me. If something is technically
impossible in the design tool, say so explicitly, propose the closest equivalent, and wait.
Silently delivering less is not acceptable. If you improve something, tell me in one line what you
changed and why.
0.1 The benchmarks: the hero and the teardown
The home hero is finished and approved, and the teardown's realism (its parts, lighting and
studio) is approved; its motion is being upgraded in Issue 1. Together they are the bar. Before you
show me any section, put it next to them and answer honestly:
1. Does every object look like a studio product photograph, as real as the iPad and phone in the
hero and the exploded phone in the teardown?
2. Is it alive? Does something always move, even when nobody touches anything?
3. Is it lit? Is there a key light, a rim light, soft shadows that follow the real shapes, and reflections?
4. Is it rich? Is every part of the screen worth looking at, with no dead empty zones?
5. Is it branded? Does it feel like Sam Electronics, not a template?
6. Is it clean? No clipped text, no overlaps, no leftovers, no zeros, nothing under the nav bar?
If any answer is no, the section is not done. Do not report it as done.

0.3 The motion vocabulary (so we mean the same thing)
Word Meaning
expo-out Fast start, long soft landing. Default for anything entering. CSS: cubic-bezier(0.16, 1, 0.3, 1).
ease-in-out Soft start and soft end. For camera moves, glides and scroll travel.
settle The object overshoots its final position by 2–6px or 2–4°, then comes back. Gives weight.
Never more than one bounce.
stagger Elements in a group start one after another with a fixed gap (e.g. 60ms).
mask reveal Text rises from behind an invisible edge, as if cut out by a blade. No fade, no bounce.
blur to sharp Starts at 8–12px blur, 0% opacity, 12–20px lower; arrives sharp. Like a camera racking focus.
light sweep A soft diagonal band of light glides across glass or metal once. 20–30% white, soft edges,
never a hard bar.
camera push The whole scene scales up 2–4% slowly, as if the camera moves closer.
hold Nothing new enters, but idle life continues. Never a frozen frame.
0.4 How we work (strictly)
1. One batch at a time, in the order of Section S. Start a new chat for each batch and read
CLAUDE.md first.
2. Build each section in its own preview page ("Preview [Section].dc.html"). Never work on the full
home page until batch 10.
3. Capture at 1440×900 and 1366×768. A still image of an animated section is never proof: send
the frames named in CAPTURE.
4. Finish every section you start. If you hit your limit, stop only at the end of a numbered issue
and tell me exactly what is done and what is not started. Never leave a section half-built.
5. For every section, answer each DONE WHEN box by name, yes or no, honestly.
6. After a section is approved, write it into CLAUDE.md as LOCKED so no future round changes it.
0.5 Things I will reject immediately
• A plain white section, or plain flat yellow with nothing designed in it.
• Any product drawn as a flat rectangle, a cartoon, a bar, a stick, a blob or a grey slab.
• Any visible light shape: spotlight cones, triangles, trapezoids, hard bars of light, LED bars sliding
up and down.
• A hard horizon line where a floor meets a wall. Square or rectangular shadows. Black blocks under
objects. Dark ellipse "floors".
• Pitch-black stages. Our darks are graphite, never black.
• Dot grids, stripe textures, tiled patterns or busy circuit wallpaper behind content.
• Dust shaped like hair or fibres, dust outside the light, or big white dots.
• Text inside a device screen that touches the screen's edges, or yellow glow blobs behind
on-screen text or logos.
• Any circuit touching or crossing text, buttons, cards or images (24px clearance).

SECTION G · GLOBAL RULES · APPLY EVERYWHERE, ALWAYS
Global rules
These apply to every section, page and state, and override anything older that contradicts them.
G11–G15 are new: they are the lessons that made the teardown work. Read them twice.
G1. Realism
• Every object (phones, parts, tools, cartons, boards, devices) is a studio product photograph:
real proportions, real corner radii, real materials (glass, satin and brushed metal, PCB with solder
mask, foil, corrugated cardboard, matte plastic), key light, rim light, contact shadow, reflections.
• Real 3D where needed (Three.js, bevelled rounded geometry, physical materials, an environment
map built in code, ACES tone mapping, full device pixel ratio).
• Small parts are matte where real parts are matte. Shine only where real objects shine (glass,
polished metal).
• No real assets exist yet and none will be supplied for the design. Everything must look real by
itself. Where a photo will come later (shop, team, Google reviews), build a tasteful placeholder
frame, never a blank card or cartoon.
G2. Motion: three layers in every section
• Entrance: every section has an opening film when it enters the viewport (headline by mask
reveal or blur to sharp, objects arriving with weight). No plain fades: every entrance combines at
least two of movement, scale, blur, mask and light.
• Idle life: something always moves on its own (current flowing, light breathing ±3% over 6s, an
object turning on a slow turntable, numbers ticking, dust falling).
• Interaction: every interactive element reacts on hover and click.
• Everything replays when scrolling back. Nothing stops dead; nothing freezes. Animate transform
and opacity; pause everything off-screen; target 60fps; static fallbacks for reduced motion.
G3. Circuits
• Keep the circuits as decoration, in the hero's spirit. Keep the nav progress line.
• No single bus forced down the page. Each section designs its own circuits where they fit; some
sections may carry one long circuit through empty space.
• Current is soft flowing light streaks (80–160px gradient dashes at different speeds), never
dots; a brighter surge when something happens nearby.
• Background circuits at 8–12% opacity, empty space only. 24px clearance from all text, buttons,
cards, images and icons. No exceptions.

G11. Light and shadow: the studio rulebook (new)
• Every scene is lit like a real photo studio: a key light (large softbox, usually top-left) that shows
as a big soft rectangular reflection on glass; a fill from the opposite side so shadows are never
pure black; a warm rim light from behind that draws a thin bright edge around objects.
• Light is never a visible shape. Where it lands on a surface it makes a soft elliptical pool (a circle
in perspective), brightest under the object, falling off naturally and feathered over 30–40% of its
radius.
• Floors and walls meet with no visible horizon (a seamless cyclorama). The floor fades into the
darkness behind.

• Shadows: a contact shadow that follows the object's real outline, darkest where it touches, then
a soft penumbra fading outward, falling away from the key light. Floating objects cast lighter,
larger, blurrier shadows the higher they float. Never squares, never black blocks.
• Reflections on polished floors: sharp at the contact point, blurring and fading out within about 25%
of the object's height, at 30–40% strength.
• Test every lit scene at full screen brightness and on a dark screen. It must read on both.
G12. Atmosphere (new)
• Dust, where used: tiny round soft specks (1–2px, a few 3px), soft white to warm white (#FFFFFF to
#EDE6D8), 10–30% opacity, only inside the light zone, each drifting slowly in its own random
direction (here and there) with a very slight downward drift, sometimes twinkling as it crosses the
key light. Never fibres or hair, never in front of objects or text.
• Bokeh, where used: a few large, very soft out-of-focus discs deep in the background, drifting
slowly, 3–8% opacity.
• Grain: a fine film grain (2–3%) on fog and yellow fields so they never look flat.
G13. Data (new)
• Zero tolerance: never "0 in stock", never "AED 0", never an empty value. Every sample number is
believable and consistent across the page (the same part has the same stock and price wherever
it appears).
• Sample data is marked as sample where the owner still has to confirm it (same-day repairs,
minimum trade units, countries shipped to, warranty periods).
G14. Screens inside devices (new)
• Anything playing on a phone or tablet screen is rendered at full device pixel ratio (at least
1080×2340 for phones), razor sharp.
• Safe margins: 12% left and right, 10% top and bottom. On-screen headlines about 9% of the
screen width, never touching the edges.
• No yellow glow blobs or LED light behind on-screen text or logos. Only a subtle glass reflection
over the screen.
• Screen adverts follow the hero's ad structure: mono eyebrow, bold 2-line headline, real lit part
render, one line, a pill button with a price.
G15. One parts library: the teardown parts, everywhere (new)
The 3D parts in the teardown are the standard for every part on the whole website.
They look like literal studio photographs: real proportions, real materials, real labels and
connectors, lit with a softbox, a rim light and contact shadows. Every other place that shows a
part must show these exact models at this exact quality. No new simpler drawings, no icons
standing in for parts, no flat thumbnails.
• Build one shared parts library from the teardown models: Screen, Frame, Flex cables, Logic
board, Battery, Cameras, Small parts (side buttons, speaker, charging port, vibration motor, SIM
tray, screws), Back glass. Write it into CLAUDE.md as the single source.
• Use the library everywhere a part appears: the What we offer family cards and all three service
ads, the Showcase screen ads, the teardown's final-call chips and phone advert, Find My Part part
cards and thumbnails, deal cards, Live Board thumbnails, the hero phone-reel cards, hover tags,
cart toasts, Samsoom's answer cards, the Bench Test phone, every icon-sized thumbnail.
• Small sizes too: a 40px thumbnail is a real render of the same model at a fixed 3/4 angle with the
same lighting, not a simplified icon.

THE FEELING. A movie trailer. Not a web page that fades things in: a sequence that hits. Lights
snap on, the title slams in, the phone appears as a silhouette and is revealed by light, then breaks
open in a burst. Every step is a new camera angle. The climax is fast, loud (visually), and lands on
a golden final shot. The customer should feel excited and want to keep scrolling, like watching a
trailer for a product launch.
What is wrong now
• Weak animation: only "Take any phone apart." moves. Everything else just appears with a plain
fade. Boring.
• The phone flies too high and then drops aggressively: at step 02 a layer shoots above the
stage and is cut off under the nav bar, then the stack falls back down hard. The motion is jumpy,
not smooth.
• Out of sync: the counter says 02 / 08 and the list highlights Frame, but the part card still shows
01 Screen.
• The leader line starts from a layer hidden under the nav and ends at the wrong row.
• The dust is gone.
• The advert is boring and empty: text arrives, stops, then the next thing arrives; big empty
white areas; a small product; weak yellow-on-grey titles; the logo has no proper moment.
• The final call chips are plain grey boxes on a grey background with tiny thumbnails.
• The stats row (8 · 14 · 9,000+) is unevenly spaced: the numbers are not equally apart and the
labels do not line up.
Motion rules for this section (these override the defaults)
• No plain fades. Every element that enters combines at least two of: movement, scale, blur,
mask reveal, light. An element that only fades in is a defect.
• Rhythm like a trailer: fast entrances (200–450ms, expo-out), short holds, then the next beat.
Tension (slow) → impact (fast) → breath (slow) → impact.
• Impact frames: on big moments (title words landing, the burst, each reassembly lock) the whole
stage gets a micro camera shake (2–3px, 120ms) and a quick 2% scale punch that settles.
• The stage box: the phone and every layer always stay inside a fixed box in the centre zone, from
48px below the nav to 48px above the bottom of the screen. No layer may ever leave it, go under
the nav, or be cut off. If the exploded stack is too tall, it is scaled to fit.
• Smooth camera: all phone and stack movement between steps uses ease-in-out over
800–1000ms along a smooth curve. No up-then-down, no drops, no overshoot above 6px.
• Always in sync: the counter, the list's active row, the part card and the leader line change
together in the same frame. Never out of step.
