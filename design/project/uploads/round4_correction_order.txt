Why this document exists
I have reviewed the entire home page from top to bottom. I am going to be direct, because polite
feedback has not worked: the home hero is the only section that is finished. It is realistic, alive,
lit, branded and rich. Almost everything after it is not. Sections are white, flat, empty, cartoonish,
static, or look like generic AI output. Several of my earlier instructions were half-applied, applied in
the wrong section, or replaced with ideas I never asked for.
This document lists 18 numbered issues, each with what is wrong, exactly what you must do, what
you must not do, and a Done when checklist. A section is only finished when every box in its
checklist is true. Not most. Every one.
Non-negotiable: do not "interpret" these instructions into something easier. Do not swap my
ideas for your own without asking. Do not skip a line because it is hard. If something is technically
impossible in the design tool, say so explicitly and propose the closest equivalent, then
wait. Silently delivering less is not acceptable.
0.1 The benchmark: the home hero
Every single section must reach the level of the home hero. Before you show me any section, put it
next to the hero and ask:
1. Does it look as realistic as the iPad and phone in the hero?
2. Is it as alive? Is something always moving, even when I am not touching anything?
3. Is it as rich? Is there something worth looking at in every part of the screen, with no dead empty
areas?
4. Is it as branded? Does it feel like Sam Electronics, not like a template?
5. Is it as polished? No clipped text, no overlaps, no leftover placeholders, no cheap shapes?
If the answer to any of these is no, the section is not done. Do not show it to me as done.
0.2 How we work this round (strictly)
1. Step 1: read the whole document. Then reply with a short summary of the Global Rules (Section
G) and the Environment Map (Section E) in your own words, so I know you understood. Do not
design anything yet.
2. Step 2: apply the Global Rules to the whole page (circuits, status dots, launcher, backgrounds,
leftovers).
3. Step 3: work through the issues in batches of 2 to 3, in page order (Section S lists the
batches). After each batch, stop and show me.
4. Step 4: for every animated section, show me a recording or a sequence of step screenshots
(start, middle, end of the animation). A single still image of an animated section will be rejected.
5. Step 5: for every section, confirm each Done-when box by name ("Box 1: done. Box 2: done…").
Why batches: you missed things in earlier rounds because too much was sent at once. Small

SECTION G · GLOBAL RULES · APPLY EVERYWHERE, ALWAYS
Global rules
These apply to every section, every page, every state. They override anything in older briefs that
contradicts them. Read them twice.
G1. Realism
• Every object (phones, parts, tools, cartons, batteries, boards, devices) must look like a studio
product photograph: real proportions, rounded corners where real objects are rounded, real
materials (glass, brushed metal, PCB, foil, cardboard), key light, rim light, soft shadow, reflections.
• No flat CSS rectangles pretending to be phones. No cartoon parts. No grey slabs.
• Where real 3D is needed, use real 3D (Three.js with rounded/bevelled geometry, physical
materials, environment lighting). Where you cannot reach realism, say so and structure the
section so a real image or image sequence can replace the drawing later without changing the
layout.
• Real assets will come at the build stage (product photos, 3D renders, shop photos, team photos,
Google reviews). Until then use high-quality placeholders, never cartoons.
G2. Motion: three layers, every section
• Idle motion: something always moves on its own (flowing current, breathing glow, drifting light,
rotating object, ticking numbers).
• Scroll motion: reveals, pinned scenes, count-ups, draw-ins. Every animation replays when
scrolling back.
• Interaction motion: hover, click, drag, tap. Every interactive element reacts.
• Motion has weight: ease in and out, slight overshoot, settle. Nothing stops dead. Nothing freezes.
• Performance: animate transform and opacity only, target 60fps. Respect reduced-motion (static
fallbacks).
G3. Circuits (corrected rule, read carefully)
• Keep the circuits. They are part of our identity, exactly like the home hero uses them.
• Keep the progress line in the top nav bar. Do not remove it.
• There is no single continuous bus forced down the whole home page. Each section may
have its own decorative circuits where they fit, and some sections may have one long circuit
running through empty space.
• Current moves as soft flowing light streaks (long gradient dashes), not tiny dots.
• Background circuits are subtle: no busy grids, no stripe textures, no tiled patterns behind
content.
• Circuits never touch or cross text, buttons, cards, images or icons. Minimum clearance: 24px.
No exceptions.
G4. Palette discipline
Colour Value Role
Signal Yellow #F7C032 Energy, highlights, primary buttons, current, active states. Used as
a field only where a section's environment says so.
Graphite #26282B / #3D3E3F Dark environments, cards, text on light. Never flat black.
White #FFFFFF Cards, text on dark, clean surfaces.
Fog #F2F3F4 Light environments, always with grain and light, never flat.
Status green light green, pulsing ONLY for the Open status dot.
